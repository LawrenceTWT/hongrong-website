03 — AI DRIVING ASSISTANT

A problem I saw
every day.

Traffic on the way to class, plus the constant stream of road-accident stories in Taiwan, became the starting point for a five-person capstone.

RoleTEAM LEADTeam5 PEOPLECONNECTAPI / TDXMODELRESNET-18BUILDANDROID
01 / PROBLEM

Too much information in too many places.

Navigation, traffic cameras, parking, and search lived in separate apps. Every switch added friction while attention should stay on the road.

02 / IDEA

Bring the useful parts into one driving assistant.

The idea combined live traffic, parking data, navigation, vehicle location, GPT, and voice interaction around driving tasks.

03 / NAVIGATION

Keep the route and the context together.

Google Maps supported route context while the assistant connected the driver to other information without opening another app.

04 / PARKING

Ask for nearby parking in natural language.

The product concept translated a voice request into structured queries for government open parking data.

05 / TRAFFIC

Turn open traffic data into a quick decision.

TDX data and traffic-camera imagery supported road-condition awareness instead of becoming another raw data screen.

06 / AI

Conversation was only useful when connected to the system.

GPT, speech-to-text, and text-to-speech were designed as an interface layer — not as the whole product.

07 / MODEL

A ResNet-18 model classified six traffic levels.

The result was promising, but the middle traffic levels exposed the limits of a small and uneven dataset.

WHAT DIDN'T WORK

The difficult part was not making a model run.
It was making the full system agree.

01

Dataset too small

The model did not see enough variation in real road scenes, weather, distance, and camera quality.

02

Middle levels were weak

Adjacent traffic levels looked similar, so the model confused the classes between obvious empty and obvious congestion.

03

Conversion changed the result

The .pth model had to reach Android through ONNX and TensorFlow Lite, while PyTorch and Android normalization also needed to match.

.PTH→ONNX→TFLITE→ANDROID
  • Use one normalization definition across training and Android.
  • Add, clean, and rebalance the dataset.
  • Inspect Precision, Recall, and the confusion matrix — not accuracy alone.

IF I BUILT IT AGAIN

  • 01A larger, better-balanced dataset
  • 02A clearer traffic experience
  • 03Accident notifications
  • 04Speed-camera information
  • 05Smoother, more contextual AI interaction
