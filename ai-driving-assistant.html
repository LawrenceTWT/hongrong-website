AI Driving Assistant

A five-person project that began with a daily problem: traffic information was useful, but fragmented across too many tools.

RoleTEAM LEAD / API / RESNET / ANDROID
Team5 PEOPLE
SystemGPT / TDX / MAPS / STT / TTS
LIFE PROBLEM → PRODUCT IDEA → WORKING PROTOTYPE
01The origin

The idea did not begin with AI. It began with the road to class.

I dealt with traffic every day and often saw stories about road accidents in Taiwan. I started asking whether the information drivers already needed could be gathered into one clearer experience.

The goal was not to add another dashboard. It was to reduce switching between navigation, traffic, parking, and search while keeping the driver’s task in context.

02My role

I led the team and worked across the seams where data, models, Android, and people had to agree.

  1. 01TEAM LEADPlanning, task ownership, progress, and coordination across five people.
  2. 02API / TDXTDX open-data research, Postman token tests, endpoints, and response mapping.
  3. 03DATASETCollecting, cleaning, and preparing traffic-camera images for six classes.
  4. 04RESNET-18Training, evaluation, class-level analysis, and conversion troubleshooting.
  5. 05ANDROIDConnecting the model and data services to the mobile experience.

04MODEL / REALITY

RESNET-18 / SIX TRAFFIC LEVELS

The difficult part was not making a model run.
It was making the full system agree.

Experiment range≈ 70–80%

An approximate project result, not a production claim. Class-level performance mattered more than one headline number.

L1L2L3L4L5L6Middle traffic levels were easier to confuse.

The model worked in PyTorch, but Android needed a different path — and preprocessing had to remain identical.

.PTH→ONNX→TFLITE→ANDROID
01 / DATA

Dataset too small

The model did not see enough variation in real road scenes, weather, distance, and camera quality.

02 / CLASSIFICATION

Middle levels were weak

Adjacent traffic levels looked similar, so the model confused the classes between obvious empty and obvious congestion.

03 / DEPLOYMENT

Conversion changed the result

The .pth model had to reach Android through ONNX and TensorFlow Lite, while PyTorch and Android normalization also needed to match.

What we changed

  • Use one normalization definition across training and Android.
  • Add, clean, and rebalance the dataset.
  • Inspect Precision, Recall, and the confusion matrix — not accuracy alone.
05IF I BUILT IT AGAIN

What would turn the prototype into a better product?

  2. 02A clearer traffic experience

  4. 04Speed-camera information
